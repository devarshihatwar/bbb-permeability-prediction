## Understanding the Publishing Landscape

### What is a preprint?
A preprint is a scientific manuscript shared publicly **before** peer review. It establishes priority and allows rapid dissemination. No journal fees, no peer review required.

### What is peer-reviewed publication?
A manuscript submitted to a journal, reviewed by experts, and published (possibly after revisions). Can involve fees.

### What is an open-access journal?
A journal that makes articles freely available to readers. There are three subtypes:

| Type | Author pays? | Reader pays? | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Diamond/Platinum OA** | ❌ No | ❌ No | eLife (community, $0), *PeerJ* (lifetime membership ~$150), *Scientific Reports* (some waivers) |
| **Gold OA (APC)** | ✅ Yes | ❌ No | Nature Communications ($5,000+), PLOS ONE (~$1,500), Royal Society Open Science |
| **Hybrid** | ✅ Yes (optional) | ✅ If not OA | Many traditional journals (Nature, Springer, Elsevier) |

### What is Zenodo?
An open-access repository (run by CERN) that assigns DOIs to research outputs. Free for researchers. Not a publisher — it does not do peer review.

## Practical Strategy (Prioritizing Zero-Cost Options)

### Step 1: Archive on Zenodo (FREE)
- Connect your GitHub repo to Zenodo
- Get a DOI for your code+data package
- Takes 5 minutes, costs $0
- **Result:** Permanently citable software/data DOI

### Step 2: Post a preprint (FREE)
- **bioRxiv** (most appropriate for BBB/permeability work)
- **chemRxiv** (chemistry)
- **arXiv** (cs.LG)
- Submission is free. Takes minutes.

### Step 3: Submit to a diamond/platinum open-access journal (LOW COST)
These journals publish without charging publication fees (or very low fees):

| Journal | APC | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **eLife** | $0 (community) | Highly selective, but zero-cost. Submit after preprint. |
| **PLOS ONE** | ~$1,395 (waivers available) | Broad scope, accepts solid methodology papers |
| **Scientific Reports** | ~$4,390 (waivers available) | High impact but expensive; waivers for developing countries |
| **Royal Society Open Science** | ~$1,590 (waivers available) | Solid general science journal |
| **PeerJ** | $199/year (or lifetime $299) | One-time payment for lifetime publishing |
| **F1000Research** | ~$1,500 (waivers) | Open peer review model |
| **Wellcome Open Research** | $0 (if Wellcome-funded) | Otherwise ~$1,500 |

**Recommendation:** Submit to bioRxiv first (free), then target PLOS ONE or PeerJ for journal publication (both have waiver programs).

### Step 4: Traditional closed-access journal (IF you have institutional access)
If your institution has a Springer/Nature/Elsevier deal, you may publish at no direct cost. Check your library's transformative agreements.

## What NOT to Do

| ❌ DON'T | Why |
|---|---|
| Predatory journals | They charge APCs but don't provide real peer review; may harm your reputation |
| Bealls List "safe" publishers | Verify via DOAJ (Directory of Open Access Journals): https://doaj.org |
| Pay high APCs without checking | Many journals have waiver programs — always ask first |
| Submit to journals outside scope | Read aims/scope carefully; rejection wastes time |
| Skip preprint | Preprints establish priority; you lose nothing by posting first |

## Journal Recommendations (by Cost)

### Zero-cost options:
- **eLife** (community-run, zero APC, highly selective)
- **PeerJ** (lifetime membership: $299 once, then free publishing)
- **F1000Research** (if eligible; open peer review)
- **Research Square** (immediate posting, not a journal)

### Low-cost options (<$1,000):
- **PeerJ** (membership: $199/year or $299 lifetime)
- **PeerJ Computer Science** ($299/year or $499 lifetime)
- **Cureus** (~$300, open, less selective)

### Mid-cost options ($1,000–$2,000):
- **PLOS ONE** ($1,395, waivers available)
- **Royal Society Open Science** ($1,590, waivers available)
- **Scientific Reports** ($4,390, waivers available — but check first)

### Waiver programs
Most major publishers (Elsevier, Springer, Wiley, Taylor & Francis, PLOS) offer fee waivers for:
- Researchers in developing countries
- Early-career researchers without funding
- Authors submitting to fully open journals from institutions without OA funds

**Always request a waiver before paying.** The worst they can say is no.

---

## How to Apply for Waivers

1. **Prepare your case:** Institution, country (Research4Life eligibility), funding status
2. **Contact the journal's customer service** (before or at submission)
3. **Request a full or partial fee waiver** — many journals offer 50–100%
4. **Document everything:** Keep email confirmations

### Quick check for developing country eligibility:
- https://www.research4life.org/
- If your country is on the Research4Life list, you likely qualify for free APCs

## DOI Strategy

1. **Code/data DOI** via Zenodo (connect GitHub repo → auto-creates DOI on release)
2. **Preprint DOI** via bioRxiv (assigned on acceptance)
3. **Article DOI** via journal (assigned on publication)

All three can coexist and are standard practice.
